A data structure decides how your data is arranged. Get the arrangement right and
the algorithm is obvious; get it wrong and no amount of clever code saves you.

You already use Python's built-in structures. This week you build three of the
classic ones yourself, which is the only reliable way to understand what the
built-ins are doing for you.

<Callout title="Before you start">
You need [Classes](/docs/week5/classes), because every structure here is written
as one, and [Lists](/docs/week3/lists).
</Callout>

## What you'll learn

- What "data structure" and "algorithm" actually mean, and how to choose between them
- Implement a stack, a queue, and a linked list from scratch
- Recognise the problems each one is the natural fit for
- Know when to stop writing your own and reach for `list`, `deque`, or `dict`
